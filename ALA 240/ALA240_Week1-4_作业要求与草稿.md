# ALA 240 — Week 1–4 作业要求与草稿

## 课件要点（用于作业引用）

- **Week 1:** 课程定位、个人学习目标、校园 well-being 模型；阅读重点是学生压力、AI 辅助与思维、冥想/正念。下一步包括阅读笔记和 NameCoach。
- **Week 2:** 社群规范；正念与压力；正念既是个人实践，也受文化和社群影响。阅读 Magee《The Inner Work of Racial Justice》Chapter 1，并开始 CYOA: Asking for Help。
- **Week 3:** 身份、自我认同、心理健康；区分 flourishing 与 languishing；讨论污名、You Soup、Story Exchange。CYOA: Asking for Help 反思必须联系 Week 3 内容。
- **Week 4:** 交叉性、交织身份、Academic Wheel of Privilege、健康的社会决定因素、自我照顾与社群照顾；开始 CYOA: Being a Buddy 和 Final Project proposal。

## 作业 1：CYOA — Asking for Help（60 分，2026-10-13 11:00 AM）

要求：选择一个以前从未使用过的、符合 help-seeking 的校园/社区服务（office hours 不算），实际参加并提交一张活动中的照片/自拍；写 **2 页**、12 号专业字体、1 英寸页边距、双倍行距的反思。必须回答：

1. 你平时如何看待求助？家庭/文化传递过哪些信息？结合 Week 3 内容与指定阅读，说明哪些观点与你的价值观产生共鸣或挑战；解释 “Three Self-Care Tools for Claiming Your Healing Time” 中最难的一项；联系 Success and Failure Student Podcast。
2. 你选择了什么帮助、为什么、希望达到什么目标？
3. 预约和参加过程如何？描述互动、环境、情绪/压力、归属感和障碍。
4. 之后感觉如何？是否获得所需帮助？下一步是什么？
5. 如何重新理解“提出需要”来改善自我照顾？你将怎样逐步适应求助？

## 作业 3：Final Project Proposal（Part 1，50 分，2026-10-13 11:00 AM）

需选择 3–4 个 Major Project Questions（MPQs），并回答：well-being 主题及其与课程/社群福祉/社会正义或健康公平的关系；目标群体（至少 10 人）；邀请和沟通方式；从准备到执行的互动活动流程与时间线；材料；如何用前后测或质性反馈评估影响。获批前不要执行 Part 2。

### 可提交方向的英文提案初稿（需按个人情况修改）

**Working title:** *Pause, Connect, and Ask: A Peer Well-Being Micro-Workshop*

**MPQs addressed:** (1) sharing semester learning about mental health and well-being; (2) five concepts that define the campus well-being landscape; (3) role in reducing barriers created by social determinants/“isms”; (4) what getting outside my comfort zone to influence community well-being looks like.

**Purpose and audience.** I propose a 30–40 minute interactive workshop for 10–15 students in ____________________ (student organization/residence hall/class). The workshop will normalize help-seeking and community care through a short grounding practice, a “sphere of control” mapping activity, and a resource-planning exercise.

**Course and equity connection.** Week 1’s Model of Well-Being frames success beyond academics. Week 2’s mindfulness material supports the grounding practice. Week 3’s flourishing/languishing and stigma discussion explains why students may delay support. Week 4’s intersectionality and social determinants framework will guide an accessibility check: participants identify how identity, cost, time, language, disability, transportation, and trust can shape access. The project treats well-being as both self-care and community care rather than an individual responsibility alone.

**Delivery and timeline.** Week 1: obtain instructor approval, recruit a campus partner, and adapt materials. Week 2: invite 10–15 participants and send an anonymous pre-check. Week 3: facilitate the workshop (welcome; grounding; sphere-of-control map; small-group scenario; campus-resource plan; closing commitment). Week 4: collect post-check and two open-ended comments; summarize results and share a one-page resource sheet.

**Materials and communication.** Slides or printed cards, sticky notes, pens, QR code to approved campus resources, and a short pre/post questionnaire. I will invite participants by email/organization channel, send one reminder, explain that participation is voluntary, and avoid collecting identifying or sensitive health information.

**Evaluation.** Compare pre/post confidence ratings (1–5) for recognizing stress, identifying one support resource, and asking for help. Also collect anonymous responses to “What is one idea you will use?” and “What barrier should the campus address?” Success means improved confidence plus at least three actionable qualitative insights.

**Impact.** I hope participants leave with one concrete support plan and a more equitable understanding of why access differs across identities and circumstances. I will use feedback to revise the activity before any Part 2 delivery.
